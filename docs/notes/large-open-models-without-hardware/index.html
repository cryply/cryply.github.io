Large open models without your own hardware: what privacy you really get

2026-11-20 · Boris Ivanov

Some people want to try a strong uncensored model. That does not make them criminals. Most of them simply do not want to be watched: they do not want every question logged, stored, and maybe used for training. Others are tired of a model that refuses a harmless question about security, medicine or fiction.

The clean answer is a local model on your own box. But the biggest open models do not fit on a home machine. My GB10 box has about 121 GiB of usable memory, and the models below need 300 to 600 GB. So this note is about the non-local options. There are two:

  1. A hosted service that already runs uncensored models.
  2. A rented GPU node, where you run the weights yourself.

One thing first, so nobody reads this the wrong way: privacy in both options is conditional. You send your prompts to a machine that belongs to someone else. A "no logs" service can log. Any of these services could even be a honeypot, built to collect exactly the questions people do not want to ask elsewhere. I have no evidence that any of them is, but from the outside you cannot rule it out. Only a local model on your own hardware can guarantee privacy, and even that only if the machine is offline. Inference servers and their Python or Node.js dependencies can send telemetry or update themselves, and "safe" packages in public repositories have been hijacked before. For real privacy, run the model on an air-gapped box, or at least block all of its outgoing traffic. Everything below is a trade: less privacy for more model.

I have not benchmarked the services or the models below. Prices and privacy statements are taken from the providers' own pages on 20 November 2026, unless I say otherwise. Check them before you pay.

What "uncensored" means here

Almost all of these models are abliterated: someone found the direction in the weights that produces refusals and removed it. The model is not retrained and it does not become smarter. It just stops saying no. The law does not change either: what was illegal to do stays illegal.

Things to keep in mind:

  • "No logs" is a promise, not a proof. You cannot verify it from outside, and a service that advertises "uncensored and private" is also the perfect honeypot. Do not type anything there that you could not afford to see leaked.
  • Routers pass your prompt on. NanoGPT and OpenRouter send the request to a third-party host. NanoGPT's own policy calls "zero data retention" a best-effort label based on what that host claims. Read the privacy page and its settings before you rely on it.
  • Payment can identify you. A card does. Crypto is visible on the chain.
  • Start small. NanoGPT and Venice let you try with no account or no card. Subscribe only if the answers are good enough.

Option 2: rent a GPU node and run the weights yourself

More work, but your prompts go only to a machine you rent for a few hours. That is better than a chat service, not the same as private.

Two warnings before you download half a terabyte:

  • Read the card. Three of these five need a special runtime or a fork. The GLM 5.3 file is the only one that the card says runs with normal llama.cpp, so it is the easy first try.
  • Low-bit files lose quality. Most of these are 1-bit and 2-bit quants. In my own test a 30B model broke below 4 bits. Very large models are said to survive it better, but I have not measured these. The 4-bit GLM file is the safer bet.

How much memory you need

Rough sizing: memory = parameters x bytes per parameter, plus 10 to 20% for the KV cache. BF16 is 2 bytes per parameter, FP8 is 1, 4-bit is about 0.5 to 0.6. For a GGUF file it is simpler: the file size plus the cache.

H200s in one node Memory Fits roughly
1 141 GB 70B in FP8, about 200B in 4-bit
2 282 GB 70B in BF16, about 400B in 4-bit
4 564 GB about 400B in FP8; the 455 GB GLM 5.3 file
8 1128 GB 600 to 700B in FP8, the largest open models

Rent the GPUs in one node (2, 4 or 8 on the same machine). Separate single-GPU instances cannot be joined. Many providers sell H200 only as 8-GPU nodes.

How the model is split depends on the format:

  • GGUF with llama.cpp: it spreads the layers over all GPUs it sees, and what does not fit can stay in system RAM (slower).
  • Safetensors (BF16, FP8) with vLLM or SGLang: tensor parallelism, one flag, for example --tensor-parallel-size 8.

Notes on China:

  • The H20 is the cheapest large memory on the list. It is slow at compute, but for inference of a big model memory matters most.
  • Ascend cards are not CUDA. They use Huawei's own stack, so expect extra work.
  • Registration may need Alipay or a Chinese bank card. Alibaba Cloud International takes normal cards, but its big-cloud prices are higher.
  • Your prompts and files sit under Chinese jurisdiction.

Other hardware you can rent

  • A large CPU node. Plenty of RAM instead of GPU memory. For example, AWS r5.24xlarge has 96 vCPUs and 768 GiB of RAM, enough for the 455 GB GLM 5.3 file. Spot prices for such instances can be very low in some regions; check the spot pricing page. Expect it to be much slower than GPUs, because every token has to read the weights from ordinary RAM. Mixture-of-experts models suffer less, since they read only a part of the weights per token. I have not measured it.
  • Dedicated LLM hosting. For example HOSTKEY: GPU servers with Ollama and Open WebUI pre-installed, models up to gpt-oss:120b, from 0.624 EUR per hour (RTX 5090) or 299 EUR per month (RTX 4090). Locations named on the page: the Netherlands, the USA and Russia. The page says nothing about logging. Easy to start, but it is the provider's setup, not yours, so assume some tracking.
  • The big clouds (AWS, Google Cloud, Azure): mature and reliable, but GPU instances on demand are the most expensive; spot is cheaper. See AWS on-demand pricing.
  • Ordinary web hosting, VPS or dedicated servers: technically possible, but many hosters treat sustained heavy compute as abuse, even on your own dedicated machine. Read the terms first.

Is a rented node private?

More than a chat service, less than your own box. Nobody logs your prompts by design, but the provider owns the machine and can technically look inside. On a marketplace like Vast.ai the host is some third party you know nothing about. Use a known provider if the content matters, and delete the instance and its disk when you are done.

Which one to pick

  • Just curious: a hosted service. Try the free tier or put in one dollar.
  • You want the largest model, or you do not trust any "no logs" promise: rent a 4x or 8x H200 node for an evening. That is $30 to $100.
  • You need real privacy: a smaller model on your own hardware. It is the only setup where nobody else is in the loop, and the only one that can guarantee anything.
